§01 · About

From a village in Bihar to an astrophysics lab in Israel

I'm a physicist from a village in Siwan, Bihar, India. I'm currently an M.Sc. student at Ariel University, working on mass transfer in eccentric binaries.

Before this I completed an M.Sc. in Physics at NIT Hamirpur, where I built a 150 mm Newtonian telescope, and worked with the High Energy Astrophysics Group at BHU on Swift, Fermi-LAT and NuSTAR data of AGN and blazars. I taught school students for four years (2015–2019), which shaped how I explain physics from first principles.

I believe that understanding the universe is a sacred act, for nothing is as pure as knowledge.

From the land of Litti Chokha to the international stage: curiosity knows no boundaries.

§01.1 · Where I come from

Bihar: where religion, knowledge and culture flourished together

Bihar is one of those regions of Indian civilization where religion, knowledge, and culture flourished together. It served as both the birthplace and the field of action for Buddhism and Jainism: Siddhartha Gautama attained enlightenment in Bodh Gaya and became the Buddha; Buddhist councils were held in Rajgir, Vaishali, and Pataliputra; and Lord Mahavira, born in Kundagrama near Vaishali, preached the message of non-violence (ahimsa) and attained Nirvana in Pavapuri.

The land of Magadha was home to great rulers such as Chandragupta Maurya, his mentor Chanakya (Kautilya), and Emperor Ashoka. Following the Kalinga War, Ashoka embraced the path of 'Dhamma' and propagated Buddhism to Sri Lanka and beyond. The mathematician and astronomer Aryabhata, associated with Kusumpura (Patna), presented concepts in his work 'Aryabhatiya' that included the place-value numeral system, a precise value for pi (π), discover the zero, and the rotation of the Earth on its axis.

The Mithila region in North Bihar corresponds to the ancient kingdom of Videha, which was home to figures such as King Janak, the sage Yajnavalkya, and the learned women Gargi and Maitreyi; later, the poet Vidyapati immortalized the Maithili language. According to the Ramayana, Sita was a daughter of Mithila, and Sitamarhi is believed to be her birthplace. Buxar is associated with the hermitage of Vishvamitra and the slaying of the demoness Tadaka, while Valmiki Nagar in West Champaran is believed to be the site of the hermitage of the sage Valmiki.

In the Mahabharata, the kingdom of Anga (covering the Bhagalpur-Munger region) was ruled by the exceptionally generous Karna, while Jarasandha, the powerful king of Magadha, governed from Rajgir. He is also famously known for his wrestling duel with Bhima.

Many travelers from abroad have also described Bihar: Megasthenes (from Greece, an ambassador of Seleucus) visited the Mauryan court at Pataliputra; Faxian (from China) arrived during the Gupta period; Xuanzang and Yijing (from China) studied at Nalanda in the seventh century; and Dharmasvamin (from Tibet) also traveled here in the 13th century.

The region boasts a rich and diverse linguistic tradition: alongside the official languages, Hindi and Urdu, languages such as Maithili, Bhojpuri, Magahi, Angika, and Vajjika are spoken by the common people.

Among cultural observances, Chhath Puja holds a special place; it is a four-day festival, comprising rituals known as Nahay-Khay, Kharna, Sandhya Arghya, and Usha Arghya, dedicated to the worship of the Sun God and Chhathi Maiya.

In the realm of handicrafts, Bihar is renowned for its distinct artistry, including Madhubani (Mithila) painting, the Sujani embroidery of Muzaffarpur, the Tikuli art of Patna, artifacts made from Sikki grass, the Manjusha art of Bhagalpur, and Bhagalpuri silk.

§02 · Research

When does an accretion disk form in eccentric binary?

In massive binary systems, the primary star drives a strong stellar wind, and part of it is gravitationally captured by the secondary. If the captured gas carries enough angular momentum, it does not fall straight onto the secondary's surface. Instead it settles into a transient (temporary) accretion disk around it. On an eccentric orbit this is even more interesting, because the separation between the stars changes continuously, so the way one star feeds the other changes along the orbit. A donor can lose mass as a wind that is densest near periastron, or fill its Roche lobe and overflow (RLOF) for only part of the orbit.

My M.Sc. project asks a concrete question: under what conditions does a transient accretion disk form around the accretor, and how long does it survive? For the next stage I am setting up the simulation analysis with yt, an open-source Python package.
