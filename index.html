    §01.1 · Where I come from

    Bihar: where religion, knowledge and culture flourished together

    Bihar is one of those regions of Indian civilization where religion, knowledge, and culture flourished together. It served as both the birthplace and the field of action for Buddhism and Jainism: Siddhartha Gautama attained enlightenment in Bodh Gaya and became the Buddha; Buddhist councils were held in Rajgir, Vaishali, and Pataliputra; and Lord Mahavira, born in Kundagrama near Vaishali, preached the message of non-violence (ahimsa) and attained Nirvana in Pavapuri.

    The land of Magadha was home to great rulers such as Chandragupta Maurya, his mentor Chanakya (Kautilya), and Emperor Ashoka. Following the Kalinga War, Ashoka embraced the path of 'Dhamma' and propagated Buddhism to Sri Lanka and beyond. The mathematician and astronomer Aryabhata, associated with Kusumpura (Patna), presented concepts in his work 'Aryabhatiya' that included the place-value numeral system, a precise value for pi (π), discover the zero, and the rotation of the Earth on its axis.

    The Mithila region in North Bihar corresponds to the ancient kingdom of Videha, which was home to figures such as King Janak, the sage Yajnavalkya, and the learned women Gargi and Maitreyi; later, the poet Vidyapati immortalized the Maithili language. According to the Ramayana, Sita was a daughter of Mithila, and Sitamarhi is believed to be her birthplace. Buxar is associated with the hermitage of Vishvamitra and the slaying of the demoness Tadaka, while Valmiki Nagar in West Champaran is believed to be the site of the hermitage of the sage Valmiki.

    Many travelers from abroad have also described Bihar: Megasthenes (from Greece, an ambassador of Seleucus) visited the Mauryan court at Pataliputra; Faxian (from China) arrived during the Gupta period; Xuanzang and Yijing (from China) studied at Nalanda in the seventh century; and Dharmasvamin (from Tibet) also traveled here in the 13th century.

    The region boasts a rich and diverse linguistic tradition: alongside the official languages, Hindi and Urdu, languages such as Maithili, Bhojpuri, Magahi, Angika, and Vajjika are spoken by the common people.

    Among cultural observances, Chhath Puja holds a special place; it is a four-day festival, comprising rituals known as Nahay-Khay, Kharna, Sandhya Arghya, and Usha Arghya, dedicated to the worship of the Sun God and Chhathi Maiya.

    In the realm of handicrafts, Bihar is renowned for its distinct artistry, including Madhubani (Mithila) painting, the Sujani embroidery of Muzaffarpur, the Tikuli art of Patna, artifacts made from Sikki grass, the Manjusha art of Bhagalpur, and Bhagalpuri silk.

    §02 · Research

    Transient accretion disks in eccentric massive binaries

    My current research examines how a companion star captures material from a massive donor, when the captured gas has enough angular momentum to form a disk, and how the disk evolves during an eccentric orbit.

    01

    Scientific background

    In a massive binary, the donor star can lose material through a stellar wind. The companion (accretor) gravitationally captures a fraction of this gas. If the captured material carries sufficient specific angular momentum, it can circularise around the accretor and form a temporary accretion disk rather than falling directly onto the stellar surface.

    Eccentricity makes the process time-dependent: the stellar separation and orbital velocity change throughout the orbit. The mass-transfer rate and angular momentum of the captured gas can therefore vary strongly, especially around periastron.

    02

    Research question

    Under what orbital and stellar conditions does a transient accretion disk form around the secondary, and what determines its duration and evolution?

    • When is the captured gas able to circularise?
    • How does wind-fed Bondi–Hoyle–Lyttleton (BHL) accretion compare with Roche-lobe overflow (RLOF)?
    • For what part of the orbit are disk-forming conditions satisfied?
    • How does the disk draining time compare with the orbital period?
    03

    Physical mechanisms

    ↗

    Wind accretion

    The companion captures part of the donor's stellar wind. The BHL accretion radius provides a characteristic scale for gravitational focusing.

    ⇢

    Roche-lobe overflow

    When the donor fills its Roche lobe, gas can pass through the inner Lagrange point and transfer to the companion.

    ⟳

    Angular momentum

    The specific angular momentum of the captured material is compared with the value needed to orbit at the accretor's surface.

    04

    Methods and workflow

    1. Binary parametersAdopt stellar masses, radii, orbital period, eccentricity and wind properties.
    2. Orbital evolutionCalculate the changing separation, orbital velocity and wind conditions along the orbit.
    3. Accretion estimatesEvaluate BHL capture and RLOF conditions, then estimate the angular momentum of the captured gas.
    4. Disk criterionCompare captured specific angular momentum with the Keplerian value at the accretor's surface.
    5. TimescalesCompare the disk's estimated viscous draining time with the orbital timescale.
    05

    Numerical simulation analysis — next stage

    I am learning to use yt to inspect FLASH simulation outputs. The planned analysis is to identify disk-like structures in the simulation and calculate the gas angular momentum at successive timesteps. This is a developing workflow; no simulation-derived result is claimed here yet.
