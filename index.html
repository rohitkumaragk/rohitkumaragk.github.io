    Bihar is one of those regions of Indian civilization where religion, knowledge, and culture flourished together. It served as both the birthplace and the field of action for Buddhism and Jainism.

    §02 · Research

    Transient accretion disks in eccentric massive binaries

    My current research examines how a companion star captures material from a massive donor, when the captured gas has enough angular momentum to form a disk, and how the disk evolves during an eccentric orbit.

    01

    Scientific Background

    Binary Star Systems

    More than 70% of massive main-sequence stars exist in binary systems. These systems evolve through stellar winds, mass transfer, and angular momentum exchange.

    Mass Transfer Mechanisms

    Two primary mechanisms drive mass transfer:

    • Bondi-Hoyle-Lyttleton (BHL) Accretion: The donor star releases a wind, and the accretor's gravity captures a portion of it.
    • Roche-Lobe Overflow (RLOF): When the donor fills its Roche lobe, material flows through the L1 point directly toward the accretor.

    Disk Formation Criterion

    The captured gas forms a disk if its specific angular momentum exceeds the Keplerian value at the accretor's surface:

    jA = ΩRacc², j2 = √(GMaRa)

    DiskjA/j2 ≥ 1
    Belt0.8 ≤ jA/j2 < 1
    No DiskjA/j2 < 0.8
    02

    Research Question

    Under what orbital and stellar conditions does a transient accretion disk form around the secondary, and what determines its duration and evolution?

    • When is the captured gas able to circularise?
    • How does BHL accretion compare with RLOF?
    • For what part of the orbit are disk-forming conditions satisfied?
    • How does the disk draining time compare with the orbital period?
    03

    Key Results (η Carinae-like system)

    QuantityValue
    Disk window32°–114°

    Duration~34 days

    Adopted parameters: Md = 170 M☉, Ma = 80 M☉, a = 19.74 AU, e = 0.9, P = 5.54 yr

    04

    Methods and Workflow

    1. Step 1: Define Binary ParametersAdopt stellar masses, radii, orbital period, eccentricity and wind properties.
    2. Step 2: Calculate Orbital EvolutionCompute changing separation, orbital velocity and wind conditions along the orbit.
    3. Step 3: Evaluate AccretionApply BHL capture formula and RLOF conditions. Estimate angular momentum of captured gas.
    4. Step 4: Apply Disk CriterionCompare captured specific angular momentum with Keplerian value at accretor's surface.
    5. Step 5: Compare TimescalesCompare disk viscous draining time with orbital timescale to determine disk survival.
